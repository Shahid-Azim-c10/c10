1. Company Purpose

AnyMile is the autonomous-logistics mission-assurance platform — the system of record for mission readiness, compliance evidence, auditability, chain of custody, operator and site eligibility, contingency workflows, and governance across regulated autonomous-logistics networks.

2. Problem

Autonomous-logistics deployments are being funded ahead of the mission-assurance infrastructure that makes them defensible to regulators, auditors, and prime-contractor customers.

  • State DoTs cannot prove the mission. Louisiana DoT and Texas DoT both have FAA-funded cargo programs in flight and have independently told us, this week, that mission assurance — readiness, chain of custody, eligibility evidence, contingency governance — is what they lack. Without it, the FAA reporting that defends and renews their grants is brittle.
  • Operators cannot scale BVLOS compliantly. The FAA’s BVLOS expansion raises the evidentiary bar; operators are not architected to produce auditable, per-mission compliance records at frequency.
  • OEMs ship vehicles without a governance layer. Airframe OEMs land cargo platforms into customers with no shared mission-assurance plane, leaving the customer to construct one from scratch.
  • No incumbent owns the layer. Passenger-side ATM systems and vehicle-side OEM stacks dominate aviation software. The horizontal mission-assurance layer — neutral across operators, OEMs, and jurisdictions — is unclaimed.

3. Solution

A neutral, third-party mission-assurance platform, operated as an independent venture-backed company, that:

  • Produces per-mission readiness, eligibility, chain-of-custody, and contingency evidence in formats state DoTs and the FAA can audit directly.
  • Governs operator and site eligibility across multi-operator, multi-corridor, multi-jurisdiction networks.
  • Onboards new corridors and operators as a reusable evidence pattern rather than as bespoke integrations.
  • Stays neutral by being structurally independent of any single OEM, operator, or geography — including its strategic anchor, Mitsubishi Electric.

This is consistent with the MELIC orchestration model described in MELIC’s May 15 memo: MELIC creates and validates strategic opportunities, then routes them to the right commercialization vehicle. AnyMile is the first instantiation of that model.

4. Why Now

A 12–18 month window is open and will not stay open.

  • Regulatory. The FAA’s BVLOS rule expansion is converting research corridors into operational ones — and raising the evidentiary bar.
  • Funding. Multiple state DoT grant cycles (TX and LA active; others queued) are spending against autonomous logistics right now.
  • Customer pull is live this week. Both Louisiana DoT and Texas DoT have confirmed mission assurance as the blocker. AnyMile has just been admitted to the Texas CAAT.
  • No category leader yet. Whoever becomes the default mission-assurance layer in the next 12–18 months sets the protocol, the evidence format, and the data gravity.
  • Spin-out unlock. Inside ME, AnyMile competes for budget against a global P&L and cannot, by MELIC’s own framing, be the long-term commercialization vehicle. Outside ME, it can contract, deploy, and iterate at venture speed while ME retains strategic upside.

5. Market Size

  • Beachhead — state DoT autonomous-logistics programs. ~50 US state DoTs, with the leading 8–10 running FAA-funded programs in the next 24 months. Annual platform spend per state DoT, fully deployed, is in the high six to low seven figures.
  • Adjacent — BVLOS-authorized operators. Several hundred US operators within 36 months; per-mission assurance fees compound with usage.
  • Adjacent — airframe OEM integrations. A double-digit set of cargo airframe OEMs globally; each integration is both a revenue line and a distribution channel.
  • Long-term — international airspace authorities and passenger AAM. The same mission-assurance primitive extends to passenger air mobility and to non-US jurisdictions as their BVLOS frameworks mature.

The defensible bottom-up TAM is in the low billions annually within 5–7 years; the strategic prize is owning the evidence protocol.

6. Competition

  • Vehicle-side OEM stacks. Built for their own airframes; structurally cannot be neutral, which is the buyer requirement at the state and federal level.
  • Passenger-side ATM incumbents. Optimized for crewed aviation; slow, expensive, and not architected for high-frequency low-altitude autonomous logistics.
  • Point UTM startups. Solve a slice (deconfliction, identification) but not the multi-operator mission-assurance layer.
  • Internal ME program (status quo). Slower decision cycles; harder to sign third-party operators who view ME as a competitor in adjacent categories; explicitly out of scope as a long-term commercialization owner per MELIC’s memo.

Why AnyMile wins: neutrality + first regulator/customer relationships + a build partner (C10 Labs) that can put a venture-grade team and capital structure around it in 90 days.

7. Product

  • Mission readiness & eligibility: automated checks for operator, vehicle, site, and crew eligibility against the corridor’s regulatory envelope.
  • Compliance evidence & auditability: per-mission evidence packs in formats state DoTs hand to the FAA, with chain-of-custody guarantees.
  • Contingency workflows & governance: decision rights, escalation paths, and post-incident review baked into the platform.
  • Integration kit: reusable SOW + reference architecture that compresses new DoT or OEM onboarding from quarters to weeks.

IP is contributed by MELIC at spin-out close via an assignment-and-license agreement; Mitsubishi Electric retains a perpetual royalty-free internal-use license with narrow, time-boxed field-of-use carve-outs that do not depress seed valuation.

8. Business Model

  • Per-mission assurance fee — variable, scales with operator usage.
  • Platform subscription — annual, tiered by connected operators/vehicles; sold to state DoTs and enterprise operators.
  • Integration & professional services — year-one revenue that funds the deployment team without burning seed capital on services headcount.
  • OEM revenue share — when AnyMile ships as the default mission-assurance layer with a cargo airframe.

Gross-margin profile is SaaS-like at steady state; year-one mix is services-heavy by design, then inverts as the reusable evidence pattern takes hold.

10. Financials & Capital Plan

10.1 Equity-allocation principle (from MELIC’s May 15 memo)

No fixed equity percentage is specified at this stage. Final equity allocation is determined through a negotiated term sheet based on:

  • Pre-money valuation
  • Contributed capital
  • Existing AnyMile assets (IP, software, data, relationships)
  • C10 Labs’ acceleration contribution
  • Founder-team equity requirements
  • Employee option-pool needs
  • Future financing requirements

MELCO preserves appropriate governance, information, commercial collaboration, follow-on investment, and potential acquisition or strategic-partnership rights.

  • Board (5 seats at close): 2 MELCO (incl. Satoshi Takeda), 1 C10 Labs (Shahid Azim), 1 CEO, 1 independent (jointly nominated).

  • Anti-dilution: MELCO and C10 Labs participate pro rata in seed and Series A; MELCO’s strategic stake is otherwise non-anti-diluted to keep the structure investor-friendly.
