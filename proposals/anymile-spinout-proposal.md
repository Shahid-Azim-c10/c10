## 1. Executive summary

AnyMile is positioned to become the orchestration layer for cargo air mobility — the
missing middleware that both Louisiana DoT and Texas DoT have independently
identified as a blocker to scaling FAA-funded cargo delivery programs. The fastest
path to capture that position is to spin AnyMile out of Mitsubishi Electric US
("ME") as an independent venture-backed company, jointly stood up with C10 Labs.

This memo proposes:

1. **A shared-equity structure** that keeps ME as the anchor strategic shareholder,
   gives C10 Labs a meaningful build-partner stake, and reserves enough room for the
   operating team and future investors to make AnyMile fundable at seed and beyond.
2. **A division of responsibilities** in which ME contributes IP, sponsorship, and
   first-customer leverage; C10 Labs leads commercial scale-up, team formation, and
   capital formation; and the founding team operates the company day-to-day.
3. **A 90-day execution plan** that converts the Texas / Louisiana DoT momentum into
   signed pilots before the seed round closes.

We are asking the June 4 review to approve the spin-out in principle, authorize the
term sheet negotiation between ME and C10 Labs, and confirm Satoshi Takeda as the
ME-side sponsor on the AnyMile board.

## 2. Why spin out, and why now

- **Market timing.** The FAA's expansion of BVLOS authorizations plus the
  state-level DoT grant cycles (TX, LA, and others queued behind them) create a
  12–18 month window where an independent orchestration layer can become the
  default integration point for drone-delivery operators, airframe OEMs, and state
  airspace authorities.
- **Customer pull is already live.** This week alone, both Louisiana DoT and Texas
  DoT confirmed that orchestration is the missing piece in their FAA-funded
  programs, and AnyMile has just been admitted to the Texas CAAT as a member. Both
  groups want results they can show the FAA quickly — an independent vehicle can
  contract, deploy, and iterate faster than an internal ME program.
- **Strategic fit for ME.** A spin-out lets ME retain upside and influence on a
  category-defining platform without having to carry it as a P&L line item, while
  preserving optionality on deeper integration with ME's automation, sensing, and
  power systems portfolio.
- **Why C10 Labs.** C10 Labs is a Cambridge-based AI-native venture studio with the
  operator bench, founder network, and investor relationships to take a corporate
  innovation asset from prototype to a fundable, customer-validated company. We
  have done this pattern before; we know what to do in the first 180 days.

## 3. Proposed shared-equity model

The headline principle: **ME is the anchor strategic shareholder; C10 Labs is the
build partner; the team owns enough to run hard; future investors have room to
come in at market terms.**

### 3.1 Founding cap table (fully diluted, pre-seed)

| Holder | Stake | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Mitsubishi Electric US | 40% | Contributes core IP, brand, first-customer access, sponsor capital |
| Founding team option pool (ESOP) | 40% | CEO, CTO, and first ~10 hires; deliberately oversized so the team carries seed dilution and still reads as venture-grade |
| C10 Labs (build partner) | 15% | Commercial scale-up, team formation, GTM, capital formation |
| Advisor / strategic partner pool | 5% | DoT advisors, airframe OEM partners, FAA SMEs |

The seed round is intentionally **not** pre-reserved on the founding cap table —
new investors price the round and dilute all existing holders pro rata. ME and C10
Labs participate pro rata to defend ownership.

*Illustrative post-seed (5M at 25M post → 20% dilution): ME ~32%, Team ~32%,
C10 Labs ~12%, Advisors ~4%, Seed investors 20%.*

All ME and C10 Labs shares vest over 36 months from spin-out close, with a 12-month
cliff, to align both sponsors with execution rather than transaction.

### 3.2 IP and license

- ME contributes the AnyMile codebase, trademarks, and any related patents into the
  new entity via an assignment-and-license agreement.
- ME retains a perpetual, royalty-free, internal-use license for any ME business
  unit, with carve-outs that prevent ME from competing with AnyMile in the
  third-party orchestration market.
- Any field-of-use restrictions (e.g., reserving certain industrial verticals to
  ME) should be narrow and time-boxed so they do not depress the seed valuation.

### 3.3 Governance and protective provisions

- **Board (5 seats at close):** 2 ME (incl. Satoshi Takeda as sponsor), 1 C10 Labs
  (Shahid Azim), 1 CEO, 1 independent (jointly nominated).
- **Protective provisions for ME:** consent rights on (a) change of control, (b)
  any transaction with a direct ME competitor, (c) material changes to the IP
  license, (d) issuance of senior preferred above an agreed cap.
- **Information rights:** ME and C10 Labs receive standard monthly reporting plus a
  quarterly strategic review.
- **Anti-dilution:** ME and C10 Labs participate pro rata in the seed and Series A
  to protect ownership; ME's strategic stake is otherwise non-anti-diluted to keep
  the structure investor-friendly.

### 3.4 Capital from ME

We recommend ME provide a **$1.0–1.5M sponsor commitment** at spin-out close,
structured as either (a) a SAFE that converts at the seed at a 20% discount, or
(b) primary equity at the founding valuation. This funds the first ~6 months of
operations and bridges to a priced seed led by an external venture firm with C10
Labs co-investing.

## 5. Commercial scale-up plan (C10 Labs-led)

### 5.1 First 90 days post-close

- Convert Texas DoT and Louisiana DoT relationships into **two paid pilots** under
  their existing FAA grants, scoped to deliver a public, FAA-visible result within
  6 months.
- Formalize the Texas CAAT membership into a working group seat and a co-marketing
  channel to the other CAAT members.
- Stand up a reference architecture and a reusable pilot SOW so the third, fourth,
  and fifth DoT engagements close in weeks rather than quarters.

### 5.2 Six- to twelve-month commercial targets

- 3–5 signed DoT or DoT-adjacent pilots, with at least one converted to a multi-year
  enterprise contract.
- 2 airframe OEM integration partnerships (so AnyMile is the orchestration layer
  shipped with their cargo platforms).
- ARR run-rate sufficient to support a Series A conversation in month 12–15.

### 5.3 Pricing and business model

- Per-flight orchestration fee for pilot deployments.
- Platform subscription (annual) for state DoTs and enterprise operators, tiered by
  number of connected operators / vehicles.
- Integration and professional services revenue in year one to fund the deployment
  team without burning seed capital on services headcount.

## 6. Team formation plan (C10 Labs-led)

### 6.1 Founding roles (close + 90 days)

- **CEO** — operator with FAA/DoT or regulated-infrastructure-software background;
  C10 Labs runs the search, ME has approval on the final candidate.
- **CTO / Head of Engineering** — preferred path is to retain the lead engineer(s)
  currently on AnyMile inside ME via a transition package (cash + meaningful ESOP
  grant).
- **Head of GTM / BD** — owns the DoT and OEM pipeline; hired from C10 Labs'
  network of regulated-industry GTM operators.
- **Founding engineers (2–3)** — orchestration, integrations, airspace data.

### 6.2 Compensation framework

- Below-market cash, top-quartile equity for the first ten hires, benchmarked
  against Boston / Cambridge venture-backed seed-stage peers.
- ESOP refreshes pre-Series A so dilution from the seed does not leave the team
  under-incented.

### 6.3 C10 Labs operating support

- Shared services (finance, legal, recruiting, design, brand) for the first 6
  months so the founding team can stay focused on customers and product.
- Embedded C10 operators (GTM, fundraising) as 0.25–0.5 FTE through seed close.

## 7. Capital plan

| Stage | Source | Amount | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spin-out close | ME sponsor commitment | $1.0–1.5M | Founding team, legal, first pilots |
| Seed (month 3–6) | External lead + C10 Labs + ME pro rata | $4–6M | Engineering, GTM, 3–5 pilots |
| Series A (month 12–18) | Tier-1 venture | $15–25M | Scale GTM, OEM integrations, international |

The structure is intentionally designed so the seed is priced by an independent
external lead — that protects ME from any internal-transfer-pricing scrutiny and
gives the company a defensible market valuation for downstream rounds.

## 9. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| ME internal stakeholders block IP carve-out | Narrow field-of-use carve-outs; Satoshi Takeda as exec sponsor pre-clears the path |
