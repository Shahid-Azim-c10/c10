## 1. Company purpose

**AnyMile is the autonomous-logistics mission-assurance platform — the system
of record for mission readiness, compliance evidence, auditability, chain of
custody, operator and site eligibility, contingency workflows, and governance
across regulated autonomous-logistics networks.**

---

## 2. Problem

Autonomous-logistics deployments are being funded ahead of the
mission-assurance infrastructure that makes them defensible to regulators,
auditors, and prime-contractor customers.

- **State DoTs cannot prove the mission.** Louisiana DoT and Texas DoT both
  have FAA-funded cargo programs in flight and have independently told us,
  this week, that mission assurance — readiness, chain of custody, eligibility
  evidence, contingency governance — is what they lack. Without it, the FAA
  reporting that defends and renews their grants is brittle.
- **Operators cannot scale BVLOS compliantly.** The FAA's BVLOS expansion
  raises the evidentiary bar; operators are not architected to produce
  auditable, per-mission compliance records at frequency.
- **OEMs ship vehicles without a governance layer.** Airframe OEMs land cargo
  platforms into customers with no shared mission-assurance plane, leaving the
  customer to construct one from scratch.
- **No incumbent owns the layer.** Passenger-side ATM systems and vehicle-side
  OEM stacks dominate aviation software. The horizontal mission-assurance
  layer — neutral across operators, OEMs, and jurisdictions — is unclaimed.

---

## 3. Solution

A neutral, third-party mission-assurance platform, operated as an
independent venture-backed company, that:

- Produces per-mission readiness, eligibility, chain-of-custody, and
  contingency evidence in formats state DoTs and the FAA can audit directly.
- Governs operator and site eligibility across multi-operator,
  multi-corridor, multi-jurisdiction networks.
- Onboards new corridors and operators as a reusable evidence pattern rather
  than as bespoke integrations.
- Stays neutral by being structurally independent of any single OEM,
  operator, or geography — including its strategic anchor, Mitsubishi
  Electric.

This is consistent with the **MELIC orchestration model** described in
Zafer's May 15 memo: MELIC creates and validates strategic opportunities,
then routes them to the right commercialization vehicle. AnyMile is the
first instantiation of that model.

---

## 4. Why now

A 12–18 month window is open and will not stay open.

- **Regulatory.** The FAA's BVLOS rule expansion is converting research
  corridors into operational ones — and raising the evidentiary bar.
- **Funding.** Multiple state DoT grant cycles (TX and LA active; others
  queued) are spending against autonomous logistics *right now*.
- **Customer pull is live this week.** Both Louisiana DoT and Texas DoT have
  confirmed mission assurance as the blocker. AnyMile has just been admitted
  to the Texas CAAT.
- **No category leader yet.** Whoever becomes the default mission-assurance
  layer in the next 12–18 months sets the protocol, the evidence format, and
  the data gravity.
- **Spin-out unlock.** Inside ME, AnyMile competes for budget against a
  global P&L and cannot, by Zafer's own framing, be MELIC's long-term
  commercialization vehicle. Outside ME, it can contract, deploy, and iterate
  at venture speed while ME retains strategic upside.

---

## 5. Market size

- **Beachhead — state DoT autonomous-logistics programs.** ~50 US state DoTs,
  with the leading 8–10 running FAA-funded programs in the next 24 months.
  Annual platform spend per state DoT, fully deployed, is in the high six to
  low seven figures.
- **Adjacent — BVLOS-authorized operators.** Several hundred US operators
  within 36 months; per-mission assurance fees compound with usage.
- **Adjacent — airframe OEM integrations.** A double-digit set of cargo
  airframe OEMs globally; each integration is both a revenue line and a
  distribution channel.
- **Long-term — international airspace authorities and passenger AAM.** The
  same mission-assurance primitive extends to passenger air mobility and to
  non-US jurisdictions as their BVLOS frameworks mature.

The defensible bottom-up TAM is in the low billions annually within 5–7
years; the strategic prize is owning the evidence protocol.

---

## 6. Competition

- **Vehicle-side OEM stacks.** Built for their own airframes; structurally
  cannot be neutral, which is the buyer requirement at the state and federal
  level.
- **Passenger-side ATM incumbents.** Optimized for crewed aviation; slow,
  expensive, and not architected for high-frequency low-altitude autonomous
  logistics.
- **Point UTM startups.** Solve a slice (deconfliction, identification) but
  not the multi-operator mission-assurance layer.
- **Internal ME program (status quo).** Slower decision cycles; harder to
  sign third-party operators who view ME as a competitor in adjacent
  categories; explicitly out of scope as a long-term commercialization owner
  per Zafer's memo.

**Why AnyMile wins:** neutrality + first regulator/customer relationships +
a build partner (C10 Labs) that can put a venture-grade team and capital
structure around it in 90 days.

---

## 7. Product

- **Mission readiness & eligibility:** automated checks for operator, vehicle,
  site, and crew eligibility against the corridor's regulatory envelope.
- **Compliance evidence & auditability:** per-mission evidence packs in
  formats state DoTs hand to the FAA, with chain-of-custody guarantees.
- **Contingency workflows & governance:** decision rights, escalation paths,
  and post-incident review baked into the platform.
- **Integration kit:** reusable SOW + reference architecture that compresses
  new DoT or OEM onboarding from quarters to weeks.

IP is contributed by MELIC at spin-out close via an
assignment-and-license agreement; Mitsubishi Electric retains a perpetual
royalty-free internal-use license with narrow, time-boxed field-of-use
carve-outs that do not depress seed valuation.

---

## 8. Business model

- **Per-mission assurance fee** — variable, scales with operator usage.
- **Platform subscription** — annual, tiered by connected
  operators/vehicles; sold to state DoTs and enterprise operators.
- **Integration & professional services** — year-one revenue that funds the
  deployment team without burning seed capital on services headcount.
- **OEM revenue share** — when AnyMile ships as the default mission-assurance
  layer with a cargo airframe.

Gross-margin profile is SaaS-like at steady state; year-one mix is
services-heavy by design, then inverts as the reusable evidence pattern
takes hold.

## 10. Financials & capital plan

### 10.1 Equity-allocation principle (from MELIC's May 15 memo)

No fixed equity percentage is specified at this stage. Final equity
allocation is determined through a negotiated term sheet based on:

- Pre-money valuation
- Contributed capital
- Existing AnyMile assets (IP, software, data, relationships)
- C10 Labs' acceleration contribution
- Founder-team equity requirements
- Employee option-pool needs
- Future financing requirements

MELCO preserves appropriate governance, information, commercial
collaboration, follow-on investment, and potential acquisition or
strategic-partnership rights.

### 10.2 Illustrative founding cap table (C10 Labs' opening position)

To show how the principle above could resolve. **Not a proposed lock — an
illustration to anchor the negotiation.**

| Holder | Illustrative stake | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| MELCO (strategic shareholder) | 40% | Capital + contributed AnyMile assets, IP, product concept, data, relationships, strategic positioning |
| Founding team ESOP | 40% | CEO, CTO, first ~10 hires; oversized to absorb seed dilution and remain venture-grade |
| C10 Labs (build partner) | 15% | Acceleration contribution, founder-team formation, GTM, capital formation, and ~$500K of embedded engineering services (4 dev engineers × 12 months minimum) |

Seed is intentionally **not** pre-reserved on the founding cap table — new

~32%, C10 Labs ~12%, Advisors ~4%, Seed investors 20%.*

All MELCO and C10 Labs shares vest over 36 months from spin-out close,
12-month cliff, to align both sponsors with execution rather than
transaction.

### 10.3 Capital plan

| Stage | Source | Amount | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spin-out close | MELCO investment | $500K–$1M | Founding team, legal, first pilots |
| Spin-out close | **C10 Labs in-kind services** | **~$500K** | **4 development engineers embedded for min. 12 months — orchestration, integrations, evidence packs, deployment** |
| Seed (month 3–6) | External lead + C10 Labs + MELCO pro rata | $4–6M | Engineering, GTM, 3–5 pilots |
| Series A (month 12–18) | Tier-1 venture | $15–25M | Scale GTM, OEM integrations, international |

**Combined day-one resourcing.** MELCO cash ($500K–$1M) + C10 Labs in-kind
engineering (~$500K) = **~$1.0–1.5M of effective build capital at close**,
which carries NewCo to a priced seed without burning cash on early
engineering hires.

MELCO's investment is structured as either (a) a SAFE that converts at the
seed with a 20% discount, or (b) primary equity at the founding valuation.
The seed is priced by an independent external lead, which protects
Mitsubishi Electric from internal-transfer-pricing scrutiny and gives the
company a defensible market valuation for downstream rounds.

### 10.5 Governance

- **Board (5 seats at close):** 2 MELCO (incl. Satoshi Takeda), 1 C10 Labs
  (Shahid Azim), 1 CEO, 1 independent (jointly nominated).
- **MELCO protective provisions:** consent on (a) change of control, (b)
  transactions with a direct ME competitor, (c) material changes to the IP
  license, (d) issuance of senior preferred above an agreed cap.
- **Information rights:** monthly reporting + quarterly strategic review to
  MELCO and C10 Labs.
- **Anti-dilution:** MELCO and C10 Labs participate pro rata in seed and
  Series A; MELCO's strategic stake is otherwise non-anti-diluted to keep
